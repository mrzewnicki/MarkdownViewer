VISUAL STYLE — STRUCTURAL REALISM WITH PAINTERLY RENDERING

Create a cinematic post-apocalyptic scene in a highly detailed AAA game concept-art aesthetic, combining realistic world construction and believable physical proportions with a distinctly painterly rendering language.

CORE VISUAL PRINCIPLE:

REALISTIC STRUCTURE
+ REALISTIC PROPORTIONS
+ REALISTIC OBJECT DESIGN
+ REALISTIC SPATIAL RELATIONSHIPS
+ PAINTERLY SURFACE RENDERING
+ PAINTERLY DETAIL DENSITY
+ CINEMATIC LIGHTING

The world must be physically believable and structurally realistic.
Buildings, vehicles, equipment, characters, vegetation and environments must have convincing proportions, perspective, scale and construction.

However, the image must NOT pursue photographic surface realism.

REALISM DEFINES WHAT THE OBJECT IS.
PAINTERLY RENDERING DEFINES HOW THE OBJECT IS DEPICTED.

The final result should look like a highly skilled environment artist painted a believable real-world post-apocalyptic location, rather than a photorealistic render with a paint filter applied.

STYLE:

- realistic AAA post-apocalyptic game concept art
- cinematic environmental concept art
- believable real-world architecture, vehicles, equipment and materials
- realistic proportions and physically plausible construction
- realistic perspective and spatial relationships
- strong environmental storytelling
- serious, mature and grounded atmosphere
- painterly interpretation of surfaces, textures and detail
- visible artistic simplification of material information
- organic grouping of shapes and textures
- selective sharpness and selective detail
- handcrafted visual character
- painterly rendering integrated into realistic forms
- cinematic composition with controlled visual complexity

STRUCTURAL REALISM:

- buildings must obey believable architectural principles
- vehicles must have realistic proportions and construction
- machinery and equipment must look functional and physically plausible
- characters must have believable anatomy, proportions and posture
- perspective must remain convincing
- objects must occupy space naturally
- scale relationships between humans, buildings, vehicles and environment must be believable
- destruction and decay must follow plausible structural logic
- vegetation must grow in physically believable ways
- environments must feel like real places rather than surreal compositions

PAINTERLY RENDERING:

- surfaces should be interpreted through painterly shapes rather than photographic texture reproduction
- use broad, medium and fine painted forms to describe materials
- surface information should be grouped into readable visual masses
- use irregular, organic transitions between light, shadow, color and texture
- avoid perfectly smooth digital gradients
- avoid uniformly sharp photorealistic surfaces
- allow textures to merge into broader painted shapes
- use selective brush-like variation in concrete, rust, wood, vegetation, fabric and debris
- let color and value variation communicate material instead of literal texture detail
- preserve the identity of materials without reproducing every physical imperfection
- painterly treatment should be clearly present but integrated into believable objects
- the image should feel intentionally painted rather than digitally textured

DETAIL & SURFACE RENDERING:

HIGH DETAIL MEANS HIGH ENVIRONMENTAL COMPLEXITY, NOT PHOTOREALISTIC MICRO-DETAIL.

- do not pursue photographic material fidelity
- do not render every scratch, pore, fiber, crack, brick, leaf or piece of debris individually
- simplify repetitive patterns
- simplify distant and secondary surfaces
- merge small imperfections into larger painterly masses
- use selective micro-detail only where it contributes to storytelling or focal hierarchy
- emphasize readable silhouettes and larger forms
- maintain believable material identity while reducing literal texture resolution
- preserve important structural details while simplifying secondary detail
- allow small details to disappear into painterly forms
- avoid excessive surface noise
- avoid uniformly dense texture information

MATERIAL INTERPRETATION:

CONCRETE:
- believable concrete structure
- cracks, stains and weathering suggested through broad tonal and textural shapes
- avoid hyper-detailed concrete pores

METAL:
- believable construction and reflections
- rust, oxidation, dents and wear represented through painterly color and value variation
- avoid photographic metal texture

WOOD:
- believable grain direction and construction
- simplified painterly grain and weathering
- irregular painted tonal variation

FABRIC:
- realistic clothing construction and folds
- material identity conveyed through broad value and color relationships
- avoid individually resolved fibers

GLASS:
- believable transparency, reflections and damage
- simplified painterly reflections and dirt
- avoid hyper-realistic surface rendering

VEGETATION:
- believable plant species, growth patterns and scale
- combine selected individual plants with grouped painterly masses
- simplify dense vegetation into readable shapes
- avoid hyper-detailed foliage

ENVIRONMENT:

- abandoned or partially reclaimed urban environments
- decaying concrete
- exposed brick
- rusted metal
- broken glass
- weathered wood
- damaged roads
- collapsed infrastructure
- abandoned vehicles
- improvised structures
- tall grass, weeds, ivy, moss, bushes and trees reclaiming human spaces
- strong contrast between decaying civilization and recovering nature
- evidence of previous human activity everywhere
- furniture, containers, tools, barricades, cables, scaffolding, graffiti, debris and improvised survival infrastructure
- environmental objects should feel naturally placed and historically connected to the location

PAINTERLY INFORMATION HIERARCHY:

FOREGROUND:
- realistic structure
- clearly readable objects
- moderate surface detail
- painterly textures
- selected sharper accents
- stronger material definition

MIDGROUND:
- realistic forms and proportions
- reduced texture density
- stronger grouping of surfaces
- moderate painterly simplification
- fewer individually resolved details

BACKGROUND:
- realistic large-scale architecture and environment
- simplified surface information
- broad painted masses
- softer edges
- atmospheric integration
- strongly reduced micro-detail

Do not maintain the same level of detail throughout the image.

Visual information must become progressively simplified, grouped and painterly with distance.

EDGE TREATMENT:

- important foreground structures: relatively crisp and believable
- focal objects: selective hard edges
- secondary objects: softer edges
- midground: moderate edge definition
- background: atmospheric and painterly
- vegetation should have naturally broken silhouettes
- distant architecture should partially dissolve into atmosphere
- avoid uniform razor-sharp edges
- avoid artificial photographic clarity everywhere
- avoid excessive depth-of-field blur
- use painterly edge variation to guide attention

LIGHTING:

- cinematic natural lighting
- physically believable illumination
- strong directional lighting when appropriate
- soft atmospheric scattering
- subtle volumetric light
- realistic contact shadows
- deep but readable shadows
- controlled highlights
- believable reflections
- wet surfaces may catch soft directional highlights
- practical light sources should meaningfully affect nearby surfaces at night
- light should shape the composition
- light transitions should contain subtle painterly variation
- preserve physically believable light direction and intensity

COLOR PALETTE:

- rust

- restrained warm orange and yellow accents

- restrained saturation

TONAL STRUCTURE:

- strong cinematic value composition
- large readable tonal masses
- clear foreground, middle ground and background separation
- controlled contrast
- atmospheric perspective
- detail concentrated around important narrative elements
- shadows retain meaningful information
- highlights remain controlled

- avoid crushed blacks

- avoid excessive local contrast

COMPOSITION:

- cinematic widescreen composition
- believable camera perspective
- eye-level or slightly low cinematic camera placement
- strong foreground, middle ground and background separation
- large architectural masses balanced with smaller human-scale elements
- natural leading lines from roads, buildings, cables, vehicles, tracks and light
- asymmetrical compositions
- environmental storytelling prioritized over spectacle
- characters should provide scale and narrative context rather than dominate the image
- compositions should feel deliberately framed like a game loading screen or cinematic establishing shot

ATMOSPHERE:

- melancholic
- tense
- lonely
- dangerous but not constantly chaotic
- quiet and abandoned yet visibly lived-in
- strong sense of time passing

- environmental storytelling without explicit exposition

- hyper-realistic texture maps
- photogrammetry-like surfaces
- uniform micro-detail
- excessive surface noise

- hyper-detailed foliage

FINAL STYLE BALANCE:

REALISTIC STRUCTURE: 60%
PAINTERLY RENDERING: 40%

Within the rendering itself:

STRUCTURAL / OBJECT REALISM: HIGH
SURFACE / MATERIAL REALISM: MODERATE
MICRO-DETAIL REALISM: LOW TO MODERATE
PAINTERLY INTERPRETATION: HIGH

The final image should preserve believable anatomy, architecture, perspective, scale, construction and object identity.

At the same time, textures, material surfaces, small details, vegetation density and overall visual information should be interpreted through painterly shapes, grouped forms and controlled simplification.

The result should feel like a believable real-world post-apocalyptic environment painted by a highly skilled concept artist.

NOT A PHOTOGRAPH.
NOT A PHOTOREALISTIC 3D RENDER.
NOT AN ABSTRACT PAINTING.

A REALISTIC WORLD, PAINTED WITH A DISTINCTIVE CINEMATIC CONCEPT-ART LANGUAGE.

---

GUIDELINES REGARDING VIOLENCE:

We are creating fictional content. When violent, disturbing or potentially graphic elements are present:

- keep the overall scene grounded and serious
- use environmental composition, lighting, smoke, shadow, debris or painterly rendering to reduce explicit graphic detail
- visually obscure excessive gore or graphic injury when necessary
- use silhouettes, partial occlusion, atmospheric haze and controlled framing to imply rather than explicitly display graphic violence
- redirect visual attention toward surrounding environmental storytelling when appropriate
- preserve the realism and narrative significance of violent events without making graphic detail the visual focus
- maintain the painterly rendering language when obscuring or abstracting violent elements
- use nearby contrasts of light, shadow, color or composition to emphasize important non-graphic aspects of the scene
- do not turn violent elements into exaggerated spectacle
- do not let gore or graphic injury dominate the composition
